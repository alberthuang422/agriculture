Report Highlights:   
India's sugar sector is poised for recovery in marketing year 2026/27 following multiple years of 
production deficits. Consecutive favorable monsoons have restored critical groundwater reserves in 
major producing states, enabling both area expansion and improved yields for this water-intensive crop. 
For the first time in two years, domestic production is expected to exceed consumption, marking a 
significant shift in supply dynamics. India's sugar consumption continues evolving along income-
stratified patterns, with commercial and institutional buyers now dominating demand while affluent 
urban households increasingly shift toward alternative healthy sweeteners. Although total sugar imports 
are expected to remain steady for MY 2026/27, the expected recovery in production should provide 
India greater flexibility to meet domestic needs, fulfill international trade obligations, and support its 
ambitious ethanol blending targets.

Area and Production: FAS New Delhi projects sugarcane planted and harvested area will reach 
5.9 million hectares (MHa) in marketing year (MY) 2026/27, with total production expected at 
463 MMT (see Table 1). This represents a two percent increase in both area and output 
compared to the current year, driven by the cumulative benefits of consecutive favorable 
monsoons in 2024 and 2025 (see Figure 1). These weather patterns replenished groundwater 
aquifers and surface water reservoirs in the primary producing states of Maharashtra, Uttar 
Pradesh, and Karnataka—addressing the fundamental constraint for this water-intensive crop. 
The two-year sequence of above-normal rainfall has restored irrigation capacity that was 
severely depleted during the 2023 El Niño event. Field surveys conducted in March 2026 across 
Maharashtra's sugarcane belt confirmed robust crop establishment and healthy vegetative 
growth, indicating farmers capitalized on improved water availability to expand planting. The 
projected sugar recovery rate of 9.2 percent reflects enhanced sucrose accumulation resulting 
from adequate soil moisture throughout critical growth stages. Government price support 
mechanisms, including the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) system and ethanol procurement 
guarantees under the National Biofuel Policy, continue incentivizing sugarcane cultivation over 
competing crops such as cotton and pulses.

Production: FAS New Delhi forecasts total sugar production for MY 2026/27 at 33.6 MMT on a 
raw value basis, comprising approximately 31.4 MMT of crystal sugar and 62,000 MT of 
khandsari.2 This projection represents a 12 percent increase from the current year's revised 
estimate of 30 MMT. The forecast is grounded in field survey observations conducted in March 
2026 across Maharashtra's primary sugarcane-growing districts, which documented healthy crop 
stands and favorable growing conditions (see Figure 3).

The projected 12 percent production increase is attributable to two consecutive years of 
favorable monsoons that restored groundwater levels and surface water availability in major 
producing regions. This directly addresses sugarcane's intensive water requirements—the 
primary constraint on cultivation and yield. The marginal improvement in sugar recovery rate to 
9.2 percent, up from 8.3 percent in the current year, reflects enhanced sucrose accumulation in 
well-irrigated cane fields. Adequate soil moisture throughout vegetative and maturation phases 
enables higher sugar content at harvest. 
Crushing operations are scheduled to commence in October 2026 following normal seasonal 
patterns. The forecast assumes continued favorable weather, operational continuity at processing 
facilities, and stable policy support through the Fair and Remunerative Price mechanism. 
Khandsari and jaggery production is expected to increase 3 percent compared to the current year, 
driven by growing consumer awareness of unrefined sweeteners and shifting preferences toward 
traditional products perceived as healthier alternatives to refined sugar.

Consumption: FAS New Delhi forecasts centrifugal sugar consumption for MY 2026/27 at 31 
MMT on a raw value basis (29 MMT crystal value), representing a three percent increase from 
the current year's estimate. For the first time in two years, domestic production in MY 2026/27 
will exceed consumption levels, ensuring adequate supply availability. 
India's sugar market is experiencing fundamental structural changes. Income-stratified 
consumption patterns now define the market, with commercial buyers increasingly dominating 
demand. Meanwhile, India has become the world's second-largest diabetic population after 
China, driven by changing lifestyles and diets. This rising diabetes prevalence is driving 
consumers to replace sugar with zero-calorie sweeteners. 
Although per capita consumption has stabilized at approximately 40 pounds (18 kilograms) 
annually, aggregate demand continues to grow, driven predominantly by commercial and 
institutional buyers rather than household consumers. Commercial demand now comprises 70 
percent of total consumption and continues to expand across soft drinks, baked goods, 
confectionery, and dairy products. Professional catering services have grown substantially in 
recent years, fueling increased commercial sugar requirements. India's extensive and 
predominantly informal catering industry—vital to community celebrations and cultural 
events—represents one of the largest sugar-consuming sectors. 
Household consumption accounts for 30 percent of total sugar usage and has declined gradually 
in recent years, particularly among affluent urban demographics. India's sugar consumption 
demonstrates pronounced stratification across economic segments, reflecting varying levels of 
health consciousness, economic capacity, and dietary preferences. Urban consumers are 
curtailing direct sugar consumption and shifting toward substitutes such as jaggery, khandsari, 
and products with minimal or zero added sugar, influenced by public health initiatives and 
evolving lifestyles. Middle-class households sustain consistent consumption of sugar-containing 
products, motivated by cost considerations, flavor preferences, and broad accessibility of 
processed foods. This segment forms the dependable foundation of consumer sugar demand. 
Lower-income and rural or semi-urban consumers remain cost-conscious and preference-driven, 
demonstrating stable sugar usage while maintaining conventional dietary habits. 
The current year's consumption has been adjusted to 30.1 MMT on a raw value basis (28 MMT 
crystal value basis), a three percent decline from the previous estimate of 31 MMT. This marks 
the second consecutive year where demand is forecast to outpace domestic supply. Concurrently, 
the Indian government established the sugar sales allocation for 2025/26 at a level three percent 
lower than the quota set for 2024/25. That previous year's allocation had already been reduced by 
five percent compared to the year before. Given that these government-mandated sales quotas 
indicate how much sugar producers must supply to the domestic market monthly, the pattern of 
consecutive year-over-year reductions signals declining consumption trends.

The adjustment also reflects current international political tensions and their economic impacts. 
The government increased the cost of domestic and commercial liquid petroleum gas cylinders in 
March and April 2026 due to tightened supply. India's food service industry is experiencing 
significant operational challenges as ongoing conflict has constrained cooking fuel availability, 
forcing establishments to reduce menu options and implement partial closures. The fuel scarcity 
is elevating energy costs and transportation expenses, ultimately raising cooking gas prices. By 
March 2026, the shortage had affected household consumers, food service establishments, and 
manufacturing facilities. Consequently, sugar demand for the current year is expected to decline. 
MY 2024/25 sugar consumption has been revised to 29.1 MMT on a raw value basis, reflecting 
adjustments made to balance trade figures. 
Trade: FAS New Delhi projects total sugar imports to remain steady at 2.4 MMT for MY 
2026/27, consisting of 2.4 MMT raw sugar and 30,000 MT refined sugar. To protect domestic 
agricultural producers, the Indian government maintains a 100 percent tariff on raw sugar 
imports. The majority of sugar entering the country arrives through the Advance Authorization 
Scheme (AAS), with a minor portion through standard commercial channels. Under AAS 
regulations, imported raw sugar must undergo refining at Indian coastal refineries prior to re-
exportation. Approximately 2.4 MMT of sugar is estimated to be re-exported under this scheme. 
Total export projections stand at 3.6 MMT, comprising 1.2 MMT raw sugar and 2.4 MMT 
refined sugar. The 1.2 MMT raw sugar export forecast assumes improved sugarcane yields in the 
2026/27 season. The anticipated recovery rate of 9.2 percent is expected to enhance sugar output 
and fulfill export requirements. This represents an eight percent increase in total exports 
compared to the current year's revised estimate of 3.3 MMT. 
For the ongoing sugar season, the Ministry of Food and Public Distribution approved exports of 
2 MMT raw sugar. Additionally, the United States granted India a raw sugar tariff rate quota 
(TRQ) allocation of 8,606 MT (raw value). The Indian government reaffirmed on April 7, 2026, 
that the country will proceed with sugar exports to meet its designated 2 MMT allocation, with 
no current intention to implement export controls. 
However, the 2 MMT raw sugar export authorization will likely be fulfilled only partially due to 
production shortfalls that have fallen below domestic demand for two consecutive years and 
higher margins in domestic prices. Industry sources have acknowledged a decline in projected 
domestic consumption for this year, as output has fallen short of previous forecasts. Export 
activity has been limited, with less than 1 MMT of refined sugar shipped due to narrow margins. 
Raw sugar exports face greater challenges as the premium required to match domestic returns is 
higher by 17 cents per pound, representing losses exceeding 2.5 cents per pound. 
Based on these factors, export figures have been revised downward to approximately 900,000 
MT of raw sugar from the authorized 2 MMT by September 2026. However, the Indian Rupee 
has depreciated 4.5 percent year-to-date to record lows, while the Brazilian Real strengthened

Prices: Fair and Remunerative Prices (FRP) for sugarcane for 2026/27 have not yet been 
announced. The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs increased the 2025/26 FRP for 
sugarcane to $41.7 per MT (INR 3,550 per MT), which was 4 percent higher than the previous 
year. State Advised Pricing (SAP) is another pricing mechanism implemented for sugarcane by 
Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Haryana, and Punjab. Unlike FRP, SAP is not tied to the sugar 
recovery rate and is typically set above the FRP. For this year, SAP for these states has not yet 
been decided. In addition to the FRP, the Indian government establishes a Minimum Support 
Price (MSP) for sugar. However, this price has remained unchanged since 2019, standing at $364 
per MT (INR 31,000 per MT), despite several adjustments in sugarcane's FRP. 
 
Cane Arrears: The average sugar recovery rate, number of active mills, and cane arrears differ 
across various states. According to industry sources, mills have disbursed 80 percent of the total 
$8.87 billion cane arrears to sugarcane farmers this season, leaving $1.79 billion in outstanding 
payments. Karnataka leads with $530 million in unpaid dues as of the current season—a sharp 
departure from the previous two fiscal years when the state had no outstanding payments. Uttar 
Pradesh shows a dramatic increase in arrears, followed by Maharashtra at $330 million. 
 
Domestic and International Price: While 2025/26 production volumes are largely finalized, the 
comparative economics of ethanol versus sugar production remain critical for 2026/27 planning. 
Recent geopolitical tensions have elevated global oil prices to approximately $100 per barrel—
the first time in nearly four years. According to industry sources, the government was already 
evaluating increased ethanol blending mandates to reduce petroleum import dependence and 
support domestic producer capacity.  
 
However, strong domestic sugar prices currently provide limited incentives for mills to redirect 
production toward ethanol. Additionally, regional variations complicate production decisions. In 
Northern India, where sugar prices exceed Maharashtra levels, diverting production to ethanol is 
even less economically viable. Substantial improvements in ethanol-to-sugar price ratios would 
be necessary to incentivize such shifts. Any ethanol policy modifications could materially affect 
India's sugar output and global market equilibrium in 2026/27. 
 
Domestic sugar prices remained firm during October-November 2025 due to festive season 
demand. Since January 2026 prices have remained near $456 per MT. With subdued global 
prices reflecting strong production expectations from Brazil, Thailand, and India, mills 
prioritized domestic sales over exports through much of the past month (see Figure 5). Following 
recent regional tensions, international pricing improved somewhat. However, raw sugar futures

Sugar prices are expected to remain elevated throughout the year. Early mill closures, and 
increased buying activity will sustain high prices while meeting domestic demand and export 
quotas. From October 2025 through mid-April 2026, average wholesale prices reached $482 per

increased demand, with additional consumption during festive seasons beginning in September.  
 
Stocks: Sugar ending stocks for MY 2026/27 are projected at 6.5 MMT, a 28 percent increase 
from the current year's estimated ending stock of 5.1 MMT. This projection is based on 
forecasted improved production and yields as groundwater levels recover. With average 
consumption, these stocks typically account for approximately three to four months of supply. 
According to industry sources, India's drive to achieve higher ethanol blending percentages amid 
projected tightening on-road fuel supplies due to current global political conditions may lead to 
reduced sugar availability for domestic markets and an increased proportion of sugar being 
converted to ethanol in the outyear. 
MY 2025/26 stock inventories are revised to 5.1 MMT, which is 41 percent lower than the initial 
estimate, reflecting the production adjustment. Unseasonal rainfall in late August through 
October resulted in decaying cane roots, thereby reducing sugar production from the initial

Policy: National Biofuel Policy and Ethanol Blended Petrol Program (EBP): Recognizing 
the essential contribution of ethanol and alternative biofuels to energy independence and 
environmental sustainability, the Indian government introduced the National Biofuel Policy in 
2018. India successfully met its goal of 20 percent ethanol blending in gasoline (E-20) during 
Ethanol Supply Year (ESY) 2025, spanning November through October. This policy framework 
established the Ethanol Blending Program (EBP), designed to expand ethanol production from 
feedstocks including sugarcane, broken rice, damaged grains, and corn (see Table 4). For the 
current year, 3.5 MMT out of total 33.5 MMT on raw value basis (remaining 30 MMT is net 
sugar production as mentioned in previous sections) is likely to be converted to ethanol. 
To encourage corn production, the government raised the corn-based ethanol incentive to INR 
71.86 per liter in ESY 2025 and sustained this rate through ESY 2026, while simultaneously 
raising rice procurement prices. Effective November 1, 2025, the Indian government authorized 
the utilization of all sugarcane-derived materials for ethanol manufacturing—including cane 
juice, syrup, B-heavy molasses, and C-heavy molasses—for the current ESY. This policy 
revision represents a substantial strategic change designed to assist sugar mills (which account 
for approximately 30 percent of ethanol output), enhance renewable energy generation, and 
accelerate India's biofuel integration objectives. 
Previously, sugar to ethanol production faced restrictions due to diminished sugarcane supplies. 
Furthermore, the government approved the allocation of 5.2 MMT of rice from the Food 
Corporation of India (FCI) to distilleries exclusively for ethanol manufacturing. According to 
FAS contacts, India has the capacity to reach a new benchmark of E-25 by ESY 2026/27, 
potentially following state-level elections.
