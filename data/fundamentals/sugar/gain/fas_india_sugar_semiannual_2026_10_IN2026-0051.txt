Report Highlights:   
India sugar production for marketing year 2026/27 is forecast to drop as erratic rainfall, 
waterlogging, and pest infestation reduce cane yields and recovery rates, widening the gap 
between domestic consumption and production. Sharp price increases—the steepest in 25 
years—have prompted duty-free raw sugar imports, inventory caps on bulk buyers, and an export 
ban to protect domestic supply.  Ending stocks for 2026/27 are forecast to decline sharply, 
reflecting weaker production, a thinner opening balance, and the continued suspension of 
imports, even as current-year stocks rise on the strength of expanded import allowances.

FAS New Delhi lowered its MY 2026/27 sugarcane planted area estimate by less than 1 percent, 
to 5.9 million hectares (HA) from the initial projection of 5.95 million HA. Planted area may 
continue to shrink slightly as a small number of farmers shift toward more profitable cash crops, 
such as arecanut, garlic, and onion, though this shift remains limited for now. While the current 
crop appears healthy, erratic rainfall added stress, reducing the production forecast to 434 million 
metric tons (MMT) — 6 percent below the earlier annual forecast (India: Sugar Annual). Post 
crop surveys conducted in Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Uttar Pradesh confirm a 1 percent 
downward revision to the harvesting estimate compared with the previous forecast, driven by 
concerns over erratic, El Niño-linked weather. 
Field surveys conducted between March and September 2026 across these states' sugarcane belts 
confirmed strong crop establishment and healthy vegetative growth, supported by two 
consecutive years of above-normal rainfall that restored irrigation capacity depleted during the 
2023 El Niño event. However, a severe rainfall deficit in 2026 is expected to reduce sucrose 
content in the cane, lowering effective harvesting area and overall cane production (Figure 1a 
and 1b). A growing water shortage poses a significant risk to sugar mill operations, as drought-
like conditions are expected to complicate milling in early 2027, compounding the pressure from 
reduced cane tonnage and sucrose content. According to the World Meteorological Organization, 
the El Niño weather pattern is strengthening and is likely to intensify further in late 2026 and 
early 2027, raising additional concerns over sugarcane production.

Area and production 
Post forecasts MY 2026/27 sugar production to decline 14 percent to 29.5 MMT on a raw-value 
basis from the annual projection of 33.6 MMT, comprising 27.6 MMT of crystal sugar and 
62,000 MT of khandsari, consistent with crop survey observations. Sugar production will likely 
remain among the lowest levels in recent years (Figure 2). To meet domestic demand, the 
Government of India approved duty-free imports of 1 MMT of raw sugar under a Tariff Rate 
Quota (TRQ) scheme through October 31, 2026.

Significant rainfall deficits and extended dry spells during critical growth periods stunted crop 
development across leading sugarcane belts, reducing both the physical weight of harvested cane 
and overall yield per hectare in major producing states (Figure 1b). These conditions also 
constrained water availability for sugarcane cultivation, contributing to lower sucrose content 
and reduced recovery rates in affected regions. Reduced moisture availability, combined with 
pressure to begin crushing earlier in the season, is expected to further lower the share of sugar 
extracted from crushed cane. Additionally, untimely rainfall in Maharashtra caused waterlogging 
in cane fields, further reducing yields. Post also observed red borer infestation in parts of Uttar 
Pradesh, linked to repeated use of the same cane variety over multiple years. As a result, the 
sugar recovery rate is projected to be 8.6 percent, with crushing operations set to begin in 
October 2026. Production of non-centrifugal sugar products, such as khandsari and jaggery, 
remains unchanged from earlier estimates. These products, used primarily for domestic 
consumption, continue to play a smaller but significant role in India's sugar sector, with 
production methods relying on the concentration of sugarcane juice through evaporation. 
Consumption 
Post's MY 2026/27 centrifugal sugar consumption forecast changed marginally, to approximately 
31 MMT (raw value), equivalent to 29 MMT on a crystal-value basis. Sugar consumption is 
forecast to exceed production by about 1.3 MMT, the widest gap in three years. Anticipating 
tighter supply, the market drove the steepest sugar price increase in 25 years. To curb record 
sugar prices, the Government of India capped inventory holding periods in August 2026 for bulk 
consumers using more than 10 MT per month at 15 days, effective September 1 through 
November 30. In response to tightening supply, the Government of India also approved duty-free 
imports of 1 MMT of raw sugar through October 31, 2026 — the country's first duty free sugar 
import allowance in nearly a decade. The sugar market is shifting toward commercial buyers, 
income-based consumption, and evolving packaging. 
Per capita use plateaued at approximately 0.18 quintals per year, with growth driven mainly by 
commercial and institutional demand. Commercial use accounts for nearly 70 percent of 
consumption (soft drinks, baked goods, sweets, dairy) and continues to rise. Catering — key to 
festivals and celebrations — remains a major, largely informal demand driver, alongside growing 
urban use of processed foods and beverages. Household use (30 percent) is declining, 
particularly among affluent urban consumers shifting to jaggery, khandsari, and zero-sugar 
options; middle-class households sustain stable demand driven by cost and accessibility; and 
lower-income consumers maintain steady, price-driven consumption. Despite steady interest in 
alternative sweeteners such as gur and khandsari among urban consumers, these products retain a 
small market share, reflecting diversifying, but not declining overall demand. Demand typically 
rises from August through November, driven by major Indian festivals, as bulk consumers such 
as sweet and confectionery makers build inventories. The broader festival season, spanning mid-
September through January, keeps sugar demand elevated across food, beverage, and sweets 
categories.

Price 
Fair and Remunerative Prices: India's Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs increased the 
Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) for sugarcane to $38.83 per MT (INR 3,650 per MT) for the 
2026/27 season, a 3 percent rise from MY 2025/26. The new rate takes effect October 1, 2026. 
To safeguard farmer income, the Government of India will waive price deductions for mills 
recording sugar recovery rates under 9.5 percent, ensuring these farmers still receive INR 3,383 
per MT. Beyond this threshold, payments will rise or fall by INR 34.6 per MT for every 0.1 
percent shift in recovery above or below the 10.25 percent benchmark. Separately, the 
Government of India also sets a Minimum Support Price (MSP) for sugar. Unlike the FRP, 
however, the MSP remains unchanged since 2019 at $330 per MT (INR 31,000 per MT). 
Exchange rate used throughout this report: 94 INR = 1 USD. 
State Advised Pricing: While most states follow the FRP framework, Uttar Pradesh, 
Uttarakhand, Haryana, and Punjab instead apply a State Advised Price (SAP) system. Unlike the 
FRP, SAP is not linked to sugar recovery rates and is generally set higher than the FRP. SAP 
rates for Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Uttarakhand stand at $44.26, $43.50, $42, and $43 
per MT, respectively. 
Cane Arrears: In the current 2025/26 sugar season, sugar mills owed sugarcane growers a 
combined INR 1,12,740 crore ($12 billion). By August 20, 2026, mills had cleared 97 percent of 
these dues, disbursing roughly $11.63 billion to farmers and leaving approximately $0.36 billion 
still pending. Metrics such as sugar recovery rates, the number of functioning mills, and unpaid 
cane balances continue to show wide variation from state to state. 
Domestic Price: Domestic sugar prices climbed sharply over the past month, rising from $515 
per MT on July 20, to $596 per MT by August 20, 2026 — an increase of nearly 16 percent. This 
sudden spike contrasts sharply with the market's longer-term stability; between August 2024 and 
July 2026, prices grew approximately 3.5 percent annually. The recent surge, therefore, appears 
to stem from short-term disruptions rather than a fundamental shift in pricing trends, as domestic 
sugar output falls short of expectations. Additionally, demand is climbing as the festive season 
approaches. 
Consumers are feeling this price pressure directly. The all-India average retail price reached $685 
per MT — up 1.77 percent from $673 per MT the previous week, 30 percent higher than a month 
earlier, and nearly 39 percent above year-ago levels. Prices vary considerably by city and region, 
ranging from a low of $428 per MT to a high of $789 per MT. Wholesale prices followed a 
similar trajectory, climbing to $637 per MT from $625 per MT over the same period — a 31 
percent increase for the month and nearly 39 percent above year-ago levels. Notably, this retail 
and wholesale climb persists even though ex-mill prices fell nearly 20 percent following the 
Government of India's move to permit duty-free imports of 1 MMT of raw sugar — suggesting a 
disconnect between mill-level pricing and prices reaching consumers (Figure 3).

International Market Price: Sugar prices on international markets are tightening in parallel, 
climbing from $475 per MT on June 30, 2026, to $554 per MT by the third week of August — 
nearly a 17 percent surge in less than two months. A projected worldwide shortfall of 
approximately 3.5 MMT for the 2026/27 season is driving this escalation, alongside weather-
driven crop losses across Europe and Asia, shrinking production in Brazil, and India's pivot 
toward tariff-free raw sugar imports. Forecasts of reduced EU sugar beet harvests add further 
pressure to an already strained global supply picture. 
Trade 
Post holds its total sugar import projection for MY 2026/27 steady at 2.43 MMT, comprising 2.4 
MMT of raw sugar and 30,000 MT of refined sugar. To protect domestic growers, the 
Government of India imposes a 100 percent tariff on raw sugar imports.1 Therefore, most raw 
sugar shipments enter the country through the Advance Authorization Scheme (AAS), with a 
small share moving through regular commercial trade. AAS rules require that imported raw sugar 
be processed at coastal refineries within India before re-export; roughly 2.3 MMT of refined 
sugar is projected to leave the country under this arrangement.  
Total exports are projected at 3.1 MMT, split between 800,000 MT of raw sugar and 2.3 MMT of 
refined sugar moving through AAS. The raw sugar export figure assumes quota-based shipments 
to the United States and European Union under TRQ arrangements. Separately, in May 2026, the 
Government of India imposed a ban on raw sugar exports through September 30, 2026, aimed at 
boosting domestic supply and keeping prices in check. 
For the current MY 2025/26 cycle, the Government of India revised its raw sugar import rules to 
permit 1 MMT of duty-free imports under a TRQ, effective through October 31, 2026. The 
policy shift followed price increases driven by concerns over a weaker production year, with the 
TRQ introduced specifically to stabilize prices. As a result, Post raised its raw sugar import

Stocks 
Post cut its MY 2026/27 ending stock forecast from 6.5 MMT to 4 MMT, a reduction of 
approximately 38.5 percent. This substantial downgrade reflects several converging factors: 
 
A weaker production year, which limits the volume of sugar entering the supply chain 
during the marketing year. 
 
As the opening stock continues to shrink, meaning the country starts the year with less 
carryover stock than previously anticipated, leaving a smaller cushion to draw from. 
 
Post assumes MY 2026/27 raw sugar imports will enter only under AAS for re-export, 
because the duty-free TRQ expires October 31, 2026, and the government has not 
authorized further allowances. Without this import relief, weaker production pulls reserve 
down. This assumption limits stock inflows, reinforcing the overall drop in ending stock, 
as constrained production, a thin opening balance, and the absence of import relief pull 
reserves down simultaneously. 
By contrast, Post raised its MY 2025/26 ending stock estimate from 5 MMT to 6 MMT, a 20 
percent increase. This upward adjustment is directly tied to a policy shift permitting the import of 
1 MMT of raw sugar under TRQ, effective through October 31, 2026. As expected, additional 
imports boosted available supply, contributing to the higher ending stock estimate. This aligns 
with the revised import volume of 3.3 MMT of raw sugar, which is likely to offset domestic 
production shortfalls, resulting in a net higher carryover than initially projected.

Policy 
National Biofuel Policy and Ethanol Blended Petrol Program (EBP): The Government of 
India rolled out its National Biofuel Policy in 2018 to strengthen energy security and 
environmental sustainability. India achieved its 20 percent ethanol-blending goal (E-20) during 
ESY 2025/26 (November–October). The policy's Ethanol Blending Program (EBP) supports 
ethanol production from feedstocks such as sugarcane, broken rice, damaged grains, and corn. In 
2025/26, an estimated 3 MMT of the 33 MMT total raw-value sugar output will be redirected 
toward ethanol, leaving 30 MMT for net sugar production, with the diverted volume accounting 
for 9 percent of total ethanol output this ESY. 
To incentivize corn-based ethanol, the Government of India raised the incentive rate to INR 
71.86 per liter for ESY 2024/25 and held it steady through ESY 2025/26, alongside increased 
rice procurement prices (Table 3). The Government of India also allocated 5.2 MMT of rice from 
the Food Corporation of India (FCI) exclusively for ethanol distilleries. Beginning November 1, 
2025, officials approved the use of all cane-based inputs — juice, syrup, and B- and C-heavy 
molasses — for ethanol production through October 2026. However, tight sugar supplies are 
expected to constrain how much molasses are diverted toward ethanol output.
