Declining Global Production 
Matches Rising Consumption 
 
 
 
 
Global consumption for 2015/16 is forecast at a record 173.4 million 
metric tons (raw value) equaling production which is forecast to decline 
for the third consecutive year.  Production is down 900,000 tons as 
reductions in the European Union, India, and the Ukraine more than 
offset gains in Thailand, Pakistan, Brazil, and Russia.  With growing 
demand, imports are forecast up 3 percent while ending stocks are drawn 
down 3.8 million tons to 40.5 million.

2015/16 Sugar Overview 
 
United States production is forecast slightly less than last year at 7.7 million tons.  Imports are 
forecast at 3.5 million tons, up 10 percent, reflecting the minimum commitment under 
agreements with the World Trade Organization (WTO) and various Free Trade Agreements 
between the United States and trading partners. This includes an increase in imports from 
Mexico of over 500,000 tons from the previous year.  Exports are unchanged, while stocks are 
drawn down nearly 4 percent as consumption grows slightly.   
 
On April 27, 2015, the U.S. Department of Commerce (USDOC) announced that it would 
resume its anti-dumping (AD) and countervailing duty (CVD) investigations against Mexico.  
The announcement was made after determining that the two sugar cane refining companies that 
filed an appeal to the agency had legal standing to do so, although they were not petitioners in 
the initial investigation.  The investigations were initiated in March 2014, but were suspended in 
December 2014 as a result of an agreement between the USDOC and the Government of 
Mexico.  The terms of the agreement limited quantities and prices of raw and refined sugar that 
could be imported from Mexico, but agreed not to impose duties that were set by the preliminary 
investigations.  The duties will not be imposed with the continuation of the investigation.  The 
USDOC is expected to release a decision by September 16, 2015. 
 
Brazil’s production is up slightly at 36.0 million tons, with less sugarcane diverted to sugar 
(forecast 43 percent) on increased profitability for ethanol production.  Additionally, the 
mandated level of the biofuel gasoline blend is up from 25 to 27 percent.  Sugar consumption is 
expected to increase slightly reflecting population growth and an expanding food processing 
sector.  Exports are projected lower for the third consecutive year to 24.4 million tons as stocks 
rise.

Thailand’s production and exports are forecast at a record 11.4 million and 8.3 million tons, 
respectively.  Records are based on higher yields and stocks drawn down to meet increased 
Asian demand, particularly from China and Malaysia.  Consumption continues to trend higher, 
driven by rising household and industrial use. 
 
Production in the European Union is forecast to drop 1.3 million tons to 15.5 million tons on 
decreased sugar beet area in response to low prices for sugar above the quota.  As consumption 
continues to trend higher, imports are projected to grow 200,000 tons to 3.2 million tons.  
Exports remain at 1.5 million tons, limited by their WTO sugar export ceiling.   
 
China’s production is projected down 180,000 tons to 10.8 million as farmers reduce area in 
response to lower floor prices, delayed payments, and higher production costs.  Imports are 
forecast 700,000 tons higher, to a record 5.5 million tons on rising consumption driven by 
continued growth in food manufacturing.  Stocks are forecast down 1.4 million tons to 5.7 
million. 
 
Mexico’s production is forecast flat at 6.4 million tons.  Exports, which are nearly all for the 
United States, are projected over 300,000 tons higher to 1.9 million.  The U.S. total is based on 
the calculation of U.S. Needs, as defined by the suspension agreements for the AD/CVD 
investigations against Mexican sugar signed in December 2014.  Consumption remains 
unchanged and stocks drop over 25 percent. 
 
Australia’s sugar production is forecast to grow 100,000 tons to 4.8 million on increased area 
and yield.  Australian sugar exports are forecast up 90,000 tons to 3.7 million on greater 
exportable supplies as consumption remains flat. 
 
Russia’s production is forecast up 150,000 tons to 4.5 million on expanded area.  Imports are 
forecast higher to meet growing consumption as well as build stocks. 
 
 
Selected 2014/15 Revisions from November Forecast: 
 
- 
Global production is up 1.9 million tons to 174.3 million. 
• India is revised up 2.2 million tons to 29.5 million as more cane is used for 
centrifugal sugar versus gur. 
• Thailand is raised 770,000 tons to 11.0 million due to favorable weather 
increasing yield. 
• China dropped 2.3 million tons to 11.0 million on reduced cane available for 
processing following weather-related damage. 
 
- 
Global imports are decreased slightly to 51.6 million.  
• Indonesia is revised down 750,000 tons to 3.1 million on stricter import policy 
enforcement. 
• European Union is lowered 500,000 tons to 3.0 million as importers are reluctant 
to pay within quota duties. 
• China jumped 1.0 million tons to 4.8 million on growing demand and lower 
production.
