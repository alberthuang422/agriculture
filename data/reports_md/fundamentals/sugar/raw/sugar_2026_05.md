United States Department of Agriculture Foreign Agricultural Service 
Sugar: World Markets and Trade 
 
May 2026 
 
India is the world’s second-largest 
sugar producer behind Brazil and 
output in 2026/27 is forecast to 
recover 3.6 million metric tons (raw 
value) to 33.6 million. Consecutive 
monsoons restored groundwater 
reserves, leading to improved yields 
and increased area. Area planted and 
harvested are forecast at nearly 6.0 
million hectares, a 2 percent increase 
from the previous year. Uttar Pradesh, 
Maharashtra, and Karnataka are the 
leading sugarcane producing states, 
accounting for nearly 60 percent of 
total sugar production. 
 
India’s sugar industry is highly 
regulated through a variety of laws and policies designed to balance the interests of growers, sugar 
mills, and consumers that prioritizes domestic stability over global market trends.  
 
Growers are shielded from market volatility by the Fair and Remunerative Price (FRP) mechanism, 
which establishes a guaranteed federal minimum price paid to growers each year, though several 
states establish higher prices. The FRP is legally-binding and sugar mills must pay growers within 2 
weeks of delivery.  
 
Sugar mills cannot sell below an established government price, which allows mills to cover costs 
and pay the FRP to growers. The government also regulates the quantity of sugar each mill can sell 
in the domestic market to prevent oversupply. Each sugar mill is assigned a speciﬁc area from 
which it must purchase all available sugarcane, and farmers in that area must sell to that mill. 
Because sugarcane loses its sugar content rapidly after being harvested, designating mills based 
on proximity to growers prevents unnecessary loss.  
 
Steep import tariffs are implemented when the government determines that domestic supplies are 
sufficient to satisfy consumption to protect growers and mills from foreign supplies priced below 
domestic supplies. Imports in 2026/27 are forecast down 200,000 tons to 2.4 million, with raw 
sugar from Brazil accounting for the vast majority.  
 
Export quotas are adjusted to ensure adequate supplies for consumers. The government provides 
highly subsidized sugar to the poorest households to ensure affordability for vulnerable 
populations. Exports are forecast up 100,000 tons to 3.6 million on increased production, with top 
destinations including Sudan, Libya, and Somalia.

2026/27 Sugar Overview 
 
Global sugar production is forecast down 
1.2 million tons to 184.9 million with lower 
production in Brazil, the European Union 
the United States and Thailand expected 
to more than offset higher India 
production. Exports are forecast down 
slightly to 62.3 million tons with reduced 
exports from Brazil, the European Union, 
Morocco, and Pakistan more than 
offsetting higher shipments from Australia. 
Ending stocks are forecast to rise slightly, 
primarily in China and India.  
 
U.S. production is forecast down slightly to 8.0 million tons. Imports are forecast nearly 600,000 
tons higher to 3.0 million based on projected quota programs consistent with World Trade 
Organization and free-trade agreement obligations, and on projected imports from Mexico, re-
exports, and high-tier tariff imports. Consumption is unchanged while stocks are reduced mostly 
due to lower output. 
 
Brazil production is forecast down 1.3 million tons to 42.5 million on the expectation that the 
ethanol/sugar mix will favor ethanol at 52 percent versus 48 percent for sugar. Strong domestic 
ethanol demand drives the shift in the ethanol/sugar mix following the August 2025 policy change 
to increase the anhydrous ethanol blend in gasoline from 27 percent to 30 percent. With lower 
sugar output, consumption and exports are forecast to decline. 
 
European Union production is forecast to fall 1.2 million tons to 14.4 million on lower sugarbeet 
area amid weak prices and high input costs, leading some growers to switch to alternative crops. 
Imports are up with lower production while exports are forecast down and consumption flat.  
 
China production is forecast nearly unchanged at 12.7 million tons on normal growing conditions 
and stable sugarcane and sugarbeet area. The central government continues to prioritize sugar 
production, with local governments in Guangxi and Yunnan provinces supporting this goal through 
subsidies and programs that promote improved planting techniques and mechanization. Imports 
and consumption are expected flat.  
 
Thailand production is forecast down 1.8 million tons to 9.5 million due primarily to reduced area 
following last year’s announcement by the Office of the Cane and Sugar Board to set the farm-gate 
price below the cost of production to reduce inflation. Consumption and exports are expected to 
remain unchanged.
